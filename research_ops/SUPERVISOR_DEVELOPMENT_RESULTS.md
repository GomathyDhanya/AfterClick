## Eight research-loop questions

1. Hypothesis: separate review adds useful decision/restoration information, while its timing changes prevention feasibility.
2. Evidence: sixvalid execution branches and a pending-access timing contrast; no safe-completion or restoration advantage over C1, and only3/6schema-valid reviews.
3. Strongest baseline: C1 matches C3's2/2safe and legitimate completions without6additional review calls across the supervised arms.
4. Why: explicit scope supports simple rules; early protection succeeds, while C2's review plus browser/enforcement overhead crosses commitment. An undispatched authorized action remains safe while withheld.
5. Confounders: only2authored sources, fixed timing laws, same-model serving variation, browser overhead, malformed-output fallbacks, shared templates and one rollout per branch. No pure latency ablation or natural-error-frequency estimate.
6. Next experiment justified? NO-GO for independent confirmation now. Further calls would mainly replicate simple rules/fallback behavior and an already observed timing mechanism. Half the reviewer outputs are rejected, so expansion would not establish a reliable incremental supervision capability.
7. Further tuning might improve interface fidelity but would test a new contract; it is not justified in this bounded wrap-up and cannot rescue a prespecified positive story.
8. Next action: finish the bounded empirical report, preserve raw failures and uncertainty, and close paid work. No new task cohort is frozen or opened.
