# **AfterClick — Research publication and execution specification**

I would restructure AfterClick as a **focused empirical study of temporal security and recoverability in autonomous AI agents**, rather than another attempt to demonstrate that a safety controller outperforms existing controllers.

The project has already accumulated extensive experimental evidence. Our priority should now be extracting verifiable scientific knowledge, identifying its boundaries, and making the entire research process independently reproducible.

I would impose three constraints:

- Every empirical claim must trace back to identifiable experimental records, executable analysis and original evidence.
- Previously collected negative, invalid and incomplete results must remain available.
- New experiments must answer specific unresolved research questions rather than merely increase the size of the benchmark.

# **1. The paper we should target**

## **Proposed title**

**AfterClick: The Temporal Limits of Safe Recovery in Tool-Using AI Agents**

*An empirical study of irreversible effects, intervention timing and legitimate task restoration in asynchronous environments.*

I would deliberately avoid claiming in the title that safety deliberation causes failures until we isolate that causal mechanism.

## **Central research question**

**How do independently progressing external effects change an AI agent's ability to prevent harm, recover from mistakes and complete authorized tasks?**

This gives us one coherent scientific problem, with implications for security, privacy, reliability and autonomous agent design.

## **Intended contribution**

The paper should establish three connected distinctions:

1. **Restoring current state is not necessarily restoring historical security.**
2. **Producing a correct safety decision is not necessarily enforcing it successfully.**
3. **Preventing unauthorized effects is not necessarily completing the legitimate task safely.**

The fourth contribution would investigate how additional deliberation interacts with all three.

## **Publication strategy**

| Target                                                   | Priority                                                              |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Empirical AI safety and agent-security research          | Primary                                                               |
| NeurIPS/ICLR/ICML empirical or benchmark-oriented tracks | Conditional on stronger validation                                    |
| ACM CCS, USENIX Security or IEEE S&P                     | Conditional on security-specific novelty and stronger causal controls |
| Relevant AI safety or systems-security workshop          | Realistic initial publication path                                    |
| Novel learned safety-controller paper                    | Separate future direction, unless a strong result emerges             |

We should not make publication conditional on our proposed controller winning. A carefully designed measurement paper can contribute valuable scientific knowledge even when its interventions fail.

## **Position relative to existing research**

The differentiation must be precise. UndoBench already examines task competence and recovery using state and historical effect evaluators. Cordon already stages irreversible effects before commitment. Runtime agent-security research already addresses interception and authorization.&#x20;

## **Proposed abstract**

## **AfterClick: The Temporal Limits of Safe Recovery in Tool-Using AI Agents**

**Abstract**

Tool-using AI agents increasingly operate in applications where background workers, concurrent users and external services continue to progress during agent deliberation and recovery. In such settings, restoring local state need not repair an irreversible external effect, and a correct protective decision can become ineffective before enforcement.

We investigate these interactions in AfterClick, an executable testbed with two constructed application environments, PostgreSQL, Chromium, asynchronous workers and independently persisted synthetic effect receivers.

Across a completed 288-branch fixed-recommendation experiment, adding 300 ms and 1,500 ms to browser enforcement increases observed forbidden-history incidence by 27.78 and 60.56 percentage points, respectively, compared with ordinary browser enforcement, conditional on twelve constructed source tasks.

In an incomplete registered LLM evaluation, forensic reports identify 65 episodes exhibiting current local completion alongside forbidden history. Observed policy outcomes additionally expose a tension between preventing unauthorized effects and completing authorized work.

Additional reviewer reasoning is associated with lower joint safe completion among observed matched primary episodes, but missing outcomes and absent causal controls prevent identifying a deliberation effect.

These findings motivate evaluating time-sensitive enforcement, immutable effect history and legitimate restoration jointly rather than grading a controller solely on its recommendation or final state. We characterize the resulting limitations without claiming universal recoverability or a generally superior safety controller.
